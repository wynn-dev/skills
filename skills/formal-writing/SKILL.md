# Formal Writing

Make formal writing sound competent, composed, and human. Prefer clarity and judgment over ornamental language.

## Workflow

1. Infer the audience, medium, stakes, purpose, and expected level of formality.
2. Choose the lightest formal register that fits the task. Formal does not mean verbose.
3. Preserve the user's meaning, facts, obligations, boundaries, names, dates, and level of certainty.
4. Draft with precise words, active phrasing, clean transitions, and varied sentence rhythm.
5. Return the ready-to-use version first. Add notes or options only when the user asks or the tradeoff matters.

## Voice

- Sound polished but not artificial.
- Use plain formal language: "use", "help", "improve", "start", "require", "because".
- Prefer concrete detail over broad claims.
- Keep sentences reasonably short. Use longer sentences only when they improve flow.
- Keep warmth restrained and specific. Do not add praise, gratitude, apology, or enthusiasm unless it serves the purpose.
- Keep accountability, requests, decisions, risks, and next steps direct.

## Avoid

- Do not use em dashes or en dashes as punctuation unless they appear in quoted text, literal data, citations, or a required template.
- Avoid AI-coded phrases such as "I hope this message finds you well", "I wanted to reach out", "delve", "leverage", "robust", "seamless", "transformative", "it's important to note", "in today's fast-paced world", and "a testament to".
- Avoid generic executive filler, inflated claims, fake specificity, and polished symmetry that makes the writing sound generated.
- Avoid unnecessary throat-clearing, excessive caveats, and long setup before the main point.
- Avoid legalistic or academic density unless the task truly requires it.

## Editing Rules

- Preserve the user's intent even when improving tone.
- Make claims no stronger than the source material supports.
- Do not invent achievements, credentials, metrics, relationships, legal terms, or commitments.
- Keep formatting appropriate to the medium: paragraphs for letters and emails, headings for reports, bullets for scan-heavy updates.
- If the user provides rough or casual text, elevate it without erasing useful directness.
- If the user's requested tone conflicts with clarity, prioritize clarity and explain only if needed.

## Final Check

Before responding, remove anything that sounds like template language, customer-support tone, or generic polish. The result should read like a thoughtful person wrote it for a real audience.
