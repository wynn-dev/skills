# Casual Writing

Make everyday messages sound like a real person wrote them. Preserve meaning, relationship, facts, commitments, boundaries, casing, spelling style, contractions, and internet shorthand.

## Workflow

1. Infer the medium, recipient, relationship, stakes, and goal. Ask only when a wrong guess would be awkward or risky.
2. Choose a natural register: usually brief, warm, and clear. Shift tone only when requested or context clearly calls for it.
3. Rewrite with simple words, contractions, active phrasing, and rhythms that fit the medium. Texts and DMs can use fragments and line breaks.
4. Return the ready-to-send version first. Give options only when tone genuinely matters or the user asks.

## Rules

- Prefer plain words: "help", "use", "start", "buy".
- Keep warmth specific and light; do not add intimacy, praise, enthusiasm, or backstory.
- Avoid stiff openings and assistant-coded phrases like "I hope this message finds you well", "I wanted to reach out", "per our conversation", "delve", "leverage", "robust", and "it's important to note".
- Never use em dashes or en dashes as punctuation in casual rewrites unless they appear in quoted text, requested style, or literal data.
- Preserve the user's casing and casual spelling. Keep "openai" as "openai" and "im" as "im" unless the user asks for cleanup or an error would confuse the recipient.
- Use emojis and exclamation points sparingly. Mirror the user if they already use them.
- Let casual writing stay a little imperfect when that feels real. Avoid over-polished, balanced, or template-like prose.
- Keep boundaries, apologies, asks, and commitments clear. Do not soften the point away.
- Before responding, check for AI slop: generic warmth, filler, fake specificity, excessive explanation, or a brand/customer-support tone. If it has that feel, cut it down and rewrite.
